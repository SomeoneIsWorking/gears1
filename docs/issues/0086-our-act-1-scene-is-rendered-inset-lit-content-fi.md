It is also a warning about everything else this table has been asked in the past -- any column-indexed reading of a resolve row taken before this commit is suspect and should be retaken.

### Note (2026-08-12)

A chain of 1/8 downsamples into one destination is a BLOOM chain, which makes this the same machinery as catalog #81 -- 'the bloom chain renders entirely black' -- and the two issues should be looked at together rather than separately. THE SCALE IS NOT IGNORED BY OUR CODE: gpu_draw.cpp:2248 passes pd.resolveScale into RT.ResolveSurfaceTo alongside the rect, the destination offset, the red/blue swap and the sampling mode, so the plumbing exists. Whether it is APPLIED the way the console applies it is not established, and that is the next question rather than an assumption -- the decode sets it from a register bias via std::ldexp(1.0f, bias), so a sign error or an off-by-one in that bias would give 8x where the console gives 1/8 and would be invisible in every unscaled pass. ### Note (2026-08-12)
CORRECTION, ONE ROUND LATER: 'RESOLVE_SCALE 0.125 IS A 1/8 DOWNSAMPLE CHAIN' IS WRONG. I read the column name and inferred a spatial scale. The code says otherwise and says it plainly: gpu_draw_resolve_decode.cpp takes bits 16..21 of R[0x231B] -- RB_COPY_DEST_INFO -- sign-extends them as a 6-bit value and computes std::ldexp(1.0f, bias), and its own comment calls it 'a signed exponent bias applied to the COLOUR on its way out of EDRAM'. That is what Xenos means by copy_dest_exp_bias: a multiplier on the colour, not a change of size. A value of 0.125 is 2^-3, so those three resolves dim their output by a factor of eight; they copy the SAME 1280x720 rectangle as every other resolve, which the rect column confirms. SO THE BLOOM-CHAIN READING IS RETRACTED and with it the claim that #86 and #81 share machinery on this evidence. They may still be related -- an exponent bias mishandled would darken a bloom chain, which is #81's exact symptom of a black bloom -- but that is a different and much narrower claim than 'these are the same downsample chain', and it rests on the bias being wrong rather than on any downsampling. Nothing here bears on the horizontal inset at all: a colour multiplier cannot move content to x 248..1012. The sign extension itself is correct on inspection: rawBias - ((rawBias & 0x20) << 1) subtracts 0x40 exactly when bit 5 is set, so 0x3D gives -3 and 2^-3 = 0.125 as measured. WHAT IS WORTH TAKING FROM THIS: the column is named resolve_scale and means resolve_colour_exponent_bias, and I inferred from the name instead of reading the derivation. Renaming it would have prevented the error.

### Note (2026-08-12)

Established this session, each independently: the guest resolves the FULL FRAME (rect 0,0 1280x720 on every resolve, read from a table whose alignment was fixed and verified); the front-buffer resolve at draw 554 copies surface 0x2d0 at scale 1.0 to 0x311000 and that destination measures FULL WIDTH; the frame ENDS at that resolve -- highest ordinal 554, zero draws into 0x2d0 after it, in two independent captures -- so the surface's end-of-frame content is exactly what the resolve read; every draw into that surface carries a full-width viewport and scissor; the present path's lookup never fails; and the present path's W x H read is correct (tested by changing it, which halved the image, and reverted). Both find the same entry -- P.surfaceTargets.find(base) -- but the present path publishes presentTarget->color while the resolve passes the whole SurfaceTarget into RT.ResolveSurfaceTo, which is free to read a different member of it: a resolved image, a second attachment, whatever the render-target cache keeps alongside .color. If those are different images, every measurement above is consistent and the inset is explained: the resolve reads the one with the frame in it and the presenter publishes another. THE CHECK IS ONE LINE AND NEEDS NO RUN: read ResolveSurfaceTo's signature and see which member of SurfaceTarget it samples. If it is .color, this issue has no remaining candidate on our side and the premise that we present the surface at all must be re-examined.

### Note (2026-08-12)
THE LAST CANDIDATE IS DEAD: THE RESOLVE READS THE SAME IMAGE THE PRESENTER PUBLISHES. gpu_draw_resolve.cpp's ResolveSurfaceTo samples srcTarget.color -- at line 208 for the blit path, 239 and 308 for the barriers, and 263 binds srcTarget.storageView, a view of that same image, for the compute path. The presenter publishes presentTarget->color. One image, both sides. So the explanation I set up last note does not exist, and EVERY candidate on our side is now eliminated: the viewport, the scene passes, the guest's rectangle, the sample grid, the present lookup, the present blit's extents, the exponent bias, late draws into the surface, and now two different images. THAT MEANS ONE OF THE MEASUREMENTS IS WRONG, and saying so is more useful than another candidate. The measurements are not equal in trustworthiness and the order to re-take them is: (1) THE RESOLVE DESTINATION PPMs, because they are decoded from GUEST MEMORY by our own decoder and a decode that mis-strides would report full width for an inset image -- they are the only evidence that the surface contains a full-width picture, and every other 'full width' conclusion rests on them; (2) the presented frame's bounding box, which is measured from raw readback pixels and is the most direct, but was taken with a luminance threshold of 5/255 that a very dark frame could fool; (3) the pass bounding boxes, same decoder as (1) and so not independent of it. THE SHORTEST PATH TO A VERDICT: dump the surface's Vulkan image directly at present time -- not its resolve destination, not the presented copy -- and measure THAT. It is the one artefact in the chain nobody has looked at, and it decides between 'the surface holds a full-width picture and something later insets it' and 'the surface never held one and the decoder has been lying about the destinations all along'.
