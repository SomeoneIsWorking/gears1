tools/oracle_lockstep.sh is built on the right idea and its header states it
correctly: both emulators count the guest's own VdSwap boundary, so indexing by
that counter makes frame N the same game moment on both sides. What it actually
did was feed the two sides two hand-written strings that were not the same walk.

    ours    f150:START, then A at f300,420,540,660,780,900,1020,1140,1260
    theirs  START@150 REPEATING EVERY 270 FRAMES, A@300 REPEATING EVERY 120

Our side stops pressing at f1260. The oracle's repeats never stop: it goes on
pressing START -- which is PAUSE once the level is up -- and A every 120 frames
for the whole run. Neither side has any movement input, so neither walks
anywhere deliberately. Two runs driven that differently are at different points
in the game at every gameplay frame, which is precisely the property the script
exists to provide.

The cost is not hypothetical. Two findings were built on comparisons made this
way and both are now withdrawn:

  * #86, "our Act 1 scene is rendered inset" -- the two frames were at different
    camera positions, and our side's "border" was rendered-but-dark geometry.
  * C023, "eight pixel shaders the console binds and we never do" -- a set
    difference over two runs that reached different points in the game measures
    how far each got. The oracle's frame 6000 has Marcus carrying a Lancer with
    312 rounds; ours is still in the unarmed prologue, which is the whole of the
    "missing HUD" that was read as evidence.

FIX. The walk is now a TABLE, in tools/menu_walk.sh, once:

    GEARS_WALK_TABLE="750:START 900:A 1050:B ... 4500:LY+ 5100:LY0 ..."

with `gears_walk_ours` and `gears_walk_theirs` GENERATING the two notations from
it, so they cannot drift again without the table changing. It is frame-keyed
because a level load stalls the frame counter on both sides while the wall clock
runs on, and it includes stick deflections so both sides actually walk.
oracle_lockstep.sh cross-checks that every frame the table names appears in both
generated strings, and PROVES that check fires on a deliberately mismatched pair
before trusting it to pass -- the first version of the check compared token
counts and flagged the correct walk as a mismatch, which is exactly the failure
mode a guard that is never exercised has.

### Note (2026-08-12)
THE WALKS NO LONGER HAVE TO MATCH. This entry is a real tooling defect and its diagnosis is correct -- the two sides were fed different input schedules, so frame N was never the same game moment. But the requirement it exists to enforce, that both sides be driven identically so their frames correspond, is no longer the only way to make a comparison. Matching on the VIEW-PROJECTION does it directly: it is guest data both emulators carry, so two frames carrying the same one are the same moment regardless of how either side got there, and the two runs may be driven completely differently or not driven at all. GEARS_DRAW_FRAME_CAMERA and tools/camera_match.py implement it and are validated in both directions. THE PROOF THAT THIS IS ENOUGH is that a camera-gated capture and an oracle run with no shared schedule resolved THE SAME 16 PASSES with none only-ours and none only-theirs -- the first paired capture to achieve that -- where every content-predicate capture before it reported structural mismatches that were really two different moments. Fixing the walk mismatch is still worth doing for any purpose that needs the two sides to follow the same route, but it is no longer a prerequisite for comparing them.

### Note (2026-08-12)
HOW WELL EACH PAIRING METHOD ACTUALLY PAIRS, MEASURED FOR THE FIRST TIME. A properly-provenanced paired capture (scratch/paircap, both sides stamped with one pair id by tools/layer_capture.sh, 234 s, gpuguard clean) lets the content selector be scored rather than trusted. Our front-buffer resolve against EVERY console front-buffer candidate in the dumped window, log-luminance correlation, best over flips and shifts to +/-64 px: f875 0.389, f876 0.493, f877 0.383, f878 0.242, f879 0.229, f880 0.228, f881 0.199, f882 0.146, f883 0.125, f884 0.117, f885 0.107, f886 0.100. Twelve candidates scored, so 'none passed' is distinguishable from 'none were tried'. THE POSITIVE CONTROL -- our frame.ppm against our own front-buffer resolve, the same metric on the same quantization -- is 0.939. SO THE BEST AVAILABLE CONSOLE FRAME REACHES 0.49 WHERE A GENUINE MATCH REACHES 0.94, and the gate is 0.60. The content selector (GEARS_LAYER_AFTER frames past the first with GEARS_LAYER_MIN_DRAWS draws, applied identically to both sides) does NOT land the two emulators close enough for a pixelwise comparison. THE SHAPE OF THE CURVE IS THE EVIDENCE THAT THE METRIC WORKS: the scores fall off smoothly and monotonically either side of f876, and the best-fitting shift grows steadily with temporal distance (dy=0 at the peak, then 8, 16, 32, 40, 40 as the frames get further away) -- exactly what a moving camera produces. This is temporal proximity being measured correctly, not noise. f876 is a genuine interior peak, not a window boundary, so the closest moment IS in the window and is still only 0.49; our frame most likely falls BETWEEN console frames and the camera moves far enough per frame that even the nearest one cannot do better. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR EVERY 'match' VERDICT layer_compare HAS EVER PRINTED: it reports MEANS, and a mean agrees between two different moments of the same scene -- on this very pair it called srcC400 f32 a match at 0.0033 against 0.0035 while the two frames correlate at 0.49. A mean-based match verdict is not evidence that the two sides rendered the same moment, and it never was. THE FIX IS THE CAMERA GATE, NOT THE CONTENT SELECTOR, and it must run against the SAME oracle run: dump the oracle's resolves AND its vs_consts in one run, then drive our side camera-gated to a constants file from THAT run, stamping both with one pair id. That is a script, not a research question.

### Note (2026-08-12)
THE CAMERA GATE MATCHES, AND THE PAIR STILL FAILS -- BUT THIS RUN CANNOT SAY WHETHER THE GATE IS THE PROBLEM, AND THE REASON IS IN THE PROFILE. First run of tools/camera_pair.sh (scratch/camerapair, one oracle run, our side gated to a FROZEN copy of that run's constants, both stamped with one pair id, 122 s, gpuguard clean, VRAM delta 0). The gate DID match: 'CAMERA MATCHED at 8.25 (threshold 10) after 273 frame(s) held', and the negative path worked properly on the way there -- at 180 frames held it reported the closest draw at 112.12 and the best ever at 112.12, at 240 frames 305.03 and 57.80, so the approach to the match is visible rather than silent. SCORED: f873 0.296, f874 0.274, f875 0.246, f876 0.213, f877 0.195, against a positive control of 0.940 and a gate of 0.60. THE PEAK IS AT f873, THE FIRST CANDIDATE IN THE WINDOW, AND THE PROFILE DECREASES MONOTONICALLY FROM IT. That is a BOUNDARY peak, not an interior one -- the opposite of the content-selected run (C043), where f876 sat inside the window with scores falling away on both sides. A boundary peak means the best-correlating console frame is OUTSIDE the dumped window, EARLIER than every frame in it. SO 0.296 IS A LOWER BOUND, NOT THE GATE'S VERDICT. The console dumped only GEARS_ORACLE_DUMP_FRAMES=5 frames, chosen by its own content selector (300 frames past its gameplay start), while our side held 273 frames hunting a viewpoint -- so the two selections land at different absolute times in the level and the console's five-frame window simply does not reach our moment. This run therefore does NOT establish that matching the view-projection to 8.25 is insufficient; it establishes that the window was too narrow to test it. DO NOT QUOTE 0.296 AS THE CAMERA GATE'S SCORE. THE NEXT RUN IS A PARAMETER CHANGE, NOT A REDESIGN: widen GEARS_ORACLE_DUMP_FRAMES so the console's window spans the moment our gate lands on, then re-score. If a candidate then passes 0.60, the camera gate is the pairing method and #62/#81 get their first valid measurement. If the peak is interior AND still below the gate, THAT is the finding that matching this view-projection to within 8.25 does not suffice -- and only then. Disk is not the constraint: five frames cost 325 MB and /home has 157 GB free.

### Note (2026-08-12)
MY OWN SCRIPT WAS KILLING THE CONSOLE AT THE START OF ITS DUMP WINDOW, WHICH IS WHY THE PREVIOUS RUN'S PEAK WAS AT THE WINDOW EDGE. tools/camera_pair.sh waited for the vertex-constants file to appear and then stopped the oracle five seconds later. Those constants land on the FIRST dumped frame, so the console was always terminated at the very beginning of its window: asking for GEARS_ORACLE_DUMP_FRAMES=45 produced 4 front-buffer frames, and the earlier run's 5. The 'widen the window' fix I proposed could not have worked, and the parameter looked as though it were being ignored. MEASURED: with DUMP_FRAMES=45 the console dumped 86 files, 4 of them front buffers. FIXED: the wait now counts the front-buffer dumps -- one per dumped frame -- against the requested count, prints progress every 60 s, and when it falls short says so AND says what a short window does to the reading ('a BOUNDARY peak in the scores means the window still did not span our moment'). The previous behaviour had no way to report that it had cut the window short, which is why it read as a parameter that did nothing. THIS ALSO EXPLAINS THE BOUNDARY PEAK cleanly and removes the alternative reading: the console's window was four frames near its own gameplay start while our camera gate held 273 frames, so the two never overlapped and the best console frame was necessarily outside the window. Nothing here says anything about whether the camera gate is a sufficient pairing key -- that question is still untested, and 0.296 remains a lower bound rather than the gate's score.

### Note (2026-08-12)
FIRST HONEST MEASUREMENT OF THE CAMERA GATE: matching the view-projection to within 9.29 gives 0.376, where a genuine match gives 0.938. Every mechanical precondition held on this run, which is what makes the number trustworthy where the two before it were not: the console dumped 60/60 frames of its window; the camera came from the shader we gate on (f3e9368c1bb68ecc, keyed by HASH now that the ordinal is known to be unstable) at guest frame 793, and the script VERIFIED that frame is inside the dumped window; provenance checked MATCH on both sides with the same frozen camera digest; and the gate matched at 9.29 against a threshold of 10 after holding 191 frames, having reported its approach honestly along the way (1141.60 at 120 frames held, 84.67 at 180). Sixty candidates scored. THE PROFILE IS COHERENT, which is the evidence this is a real result and not another window artifact: the camera was taken from console frame 793, and f793 is the winner at 0.376, with the scores decaying monotonically as the console walks away from that viewpoint -- 794 at 0.328, 795 at 0.292, 796 at 0.120, and into the noise beyond. The reference point wins, and nothing earlier could win because 793 IS the frame the camera came from. SO THE CAMERA GATE AT THRESHOLD 10 IS NOT SUFFICIENT FOR A PIXELWISE COMPARISON. It is better than the content selector on a like-for-like reading (both peak-of-window numbers: 0.49 content, but that one was an unpaired lower bound; this one is properly paired) -- what matters is that neither reaches 0.60 and the control is 0.94. TWO RESIDUALS ARE IN THAT 0.376 AND THEY ARE SEPARABLE: the camera distance itself (9.29, essentially at the threshold) and the ONE FRAME of further movement the runtime warns about, since the capture is the frame AFTER the match. THE NEXT EXPERIMENT IS A KNOB SWEEP, NOT A REDESIGN: re-run with CAMERA_NEAR tightened and see whether the score rises. If it climbs toward the control as the threshold falls, the method is sound and needs only a tighter gate and a longer run to find one; if it stays near 0.376 however tight the camera match, then the view-projection is not the whole state that determines the picture and the pairing needs a different or additional key. Either answer is worth having and one run buys the first point. CAVEAT ON THE LOW END OF THE TABLE: below about r=0.05 the 'best transform' column reports flips and 64-px shifts, which is arbitrary at that magnitude -- do not read 'horizontal flip' on a 0.01 candidate as meaning anything.
